SESSION 07 · STUDY NOTES

The TransformerEncoder

How text becomes context-aware numbers — the architecture that broke open modern AI.

2017 PAPER "ATTENTION IS ALL YOU NEED"
Every token in the input is enriched with three layers of information — what it means generically, where it sits, and how it relates to every other token. Once each token carries all the context it needs, the model can process them all at once.
§ 01 — STARTING POINT

Why text becomes numbers

Models consume numbers, not characters. Text is unstructured, so every token must be turned into a vector before anything else can happen. ChatGPT (application) wraps GPT (model), which is a Transformer implemented in a deep-learning framework.

The user-visible task — "answer my prompt" — is internally just predict the next token, repeated. But the predictions across the input are computed in parallel, not left-to-right. That parallelism is the whole reason scale became possible after 2017.

§ 02 — GENERIC EMBEDDINGS

Words as coordinates in topic-space

Take your vocabulary (~20,000 root words — jump, not jump/jumped/jumping) and pick a set of latent "topics" as axes: animal, bird, sky, machine. Every word gets a score along each axis describing how close it sits to that concept.

dog scores high on animal, low on sky. eagle scores moderately on animal but high on bird and sky. In practice the dimensionality is large (256, 512, etc.) and chosen by the architect, not fixed. Scores are constrained to sum to 1 (softmax produces this — covered later).

THE LIMITATION

These embeddings are generic. The vector for dog is identical in "man bites dog" and "dog bites man". Word2Vec carries no order and no input-specific context.

§ 03 — PROBLEM 1: ORDER

Positional encoding

"Man bites dog" and "dog bites man" share the same three words and therefore the same Word2Vec vectors, but they mean opposite things. English meaning depends heavily on position.

The fix: add a positional encoding to each token's generic embedding. Conceptually, attach a position index (0, 1, 2, …) so the two sentences yield different combined embeddings even though the words are identical. The actual encoding scheme is more sophisticated than 0/1/2 — that comes next session.

§ 04 — PROBLEM 2: RELATIONSHIPS

Attention — what looks at what

Position alone still doesn't cover "Ram is going to school. He is a good student." The model needs to know he refers to Ram. That's a relationship between tokens, not a property of either token in isolation.

The fix: for every token, attach a relationship vector — scores for how strongly that token relates to every other token in the input (including itself). For "man bites dog", the row for man might be (man:1.0, bites:0.8, dog:0.2); the row sums to 1.

This is the attention score. Unlike Word2Vec it is input-specific (contextual), not generic. This is the 2017 innovation.

§ 05 — THE SYNTHESIS

The three-layer embedding

For each token, the encoder produces three layers, added together:

generic + positional + relationship = rich embedding
LAYER 1
Generic embedding
English-level meaning (Word2Vec-style). Same in every sentence the word appears in.
LAYER 2
Positional encoding
Where the token sits in the input. Added once, before the encoder stack. Position doesn't change between layers.
LAYER 3
Relationship (attention)
How this token relates to every other token in this specific input. Recomputed at every encoder layer.

These are added, not concatenated — context is enriched without growing the dimensionality. Once every token carries all three, no token needs to wait for any other token's processing. This is what unlocks parallel execution.

§ 06 — THE STRUCTURAL BREAK

Sequential vs parallel

BEFORE 2017 — RNN / LSTM

One token at a time

  • ⚠ Long contexts decay
  • ⚠ Scales linearly with length
  • ⚠ Hard to parallelize on GPU
2017+ — TRANSFORMER

All tokens at once

  • ✓ Every token sees every other
  • ✓ Same wall-clock for 5 or 5,000
  • ✓ GPU-native

Whether the input is 5 words or 5,000, all tokens are processed simultaneously. The pre-Transformer models would effectively "forget" early tokens by the time they reached late ones — give an LSTM ten sentences and it remembers mostly the last few. Attention removed that bottleneck because every token sees every other token directly, regardless of distance.

§ 07 — ARCHITECTURE

The encoder block

One encoder block, in order:

INPUT
tokenize
EMBED
Word2Vec
POSITION
add
ATTENTION
self-attention
FFN
feed-forward

The original paper stacks this block 6 times. From block 2 onward, the input is the previous block's output, not the raw embedding — each layer refines the representation further.

Positional encoding is not repeated inside the stack; attention + FFN is the part that repeats. The number 6 isn't sacred — GPT-3.5, GPT-4, etc., use different counts. What matters is that the pattern repeats.

§ 08 — WHY DEPTH HELPS

Why repetition works

Repetition is not a Transformer-specific trick — it's how deep models capture progressively more abstract patterns.

ANN stacks hidden layers (1, 3, 5, 10…) for the same reason. CNN stacks convolution blocks; early layers catch edges, deeper layers catch parts, deepest layers catch whole objects.

Each Transformer block sees richer input than the last, so stacking lets the model learn finer-grained patterns in the text.

REMEMBER

AI = pattern recognition.

Depth gives you more patterns.

§ 09 — TWO HALVES

Encoder vs decoder

ENCODER

Represents

Takes text, outputs enriched embeddings. Not generative.

DECODER

Generates

Takes encoder output (or its own previous output), generates the next token. Architecture is ~99% the same as the encoder — the 1% difference is masked self-attention: a token can only attend to earlier tokens.

GPT is decoder-based but still "encodes" your prompt — that happens inside the decoder via self-attention. Whether you need a separate encoder depends on the task; covered later.

§ 10 — ASIDES

Worth keeping in mind

Temperature

A decoder parameter exposed in playgrounds. Low temperature → always pick the highest-probability next token (deterministic). High temperature → sample from the top-N candidates (more variation, marketed as "creativity"). It's a sampling knob on a prediction model, not a creativity dial in any deep sense.

Why your GPT ≠ OpenAI's GPT

The algorithm has been public since 2017. The gap is data and compute — months of human labeling, augmentation, RLHF-style feedback loops, plus enormous compute budgets. Algorithm is commodity; data is moat. (Hence the data-center investment frenzy and the strategic value of any pipeline that produces labeled data.)

Hugging Face

Hundreds of thousands of community-built models exist; building your own small GPT from scratch with PyTorch/Keras/TF is a tractable exercise. Don't expect production quality — the limit is data, not code.

REFERENCE

Glossary

Token
Unit the model processes — roughly a word or sub-word.
Embedding
Vector representation of a token.
Word2Vec
Algorithm producing generic, context-free word vectors.
Positional encoding
Vector added to a token's embedding to encode its location in the input.
Self-attention
Mechanism producing per-token relationship vectors across the input.
Masked self-attention
Decoder-only variant that hides future tokens during training.
Feed-forward (FFN)
The ANN sub-layer that follows attention in each encoder block.
Encoder / Decoder
The two halves of the Transformer; encoder represents, decoder generates.
LLM
A large model that "understands language" because it captures generic meaning + position + cross-token relationships.
